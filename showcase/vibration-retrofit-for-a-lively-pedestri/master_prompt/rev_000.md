## 2. Roles
Retrofit team for 40 m lively footbridge with 1.9 Hz first vertical mode. 4 members with distinct domains. All design must preserve appearance from the deck.

### Structural Dynamics Lead
- **Domain:** Structural dynamics and modal analysis
- **Responsibility:** Confirm 1.9 Hz first vertical bending mode, low damping baseline, target frequency and damping after retrofit. Owns under-deck mass/stiffness placement without altering deck sightlines.
- **Evidence duty:** Must cite evidence ids for mode shapes, frequencies, and acceleration estimates before any ready vote.

### Vibration Control Specialist
- **Domain:** Damping devices and tuned mass dampers (TMDs)
- **Responsibility:** Design concealed under-deck TMDs / viscous dampers to bring vibration to comfortable level. Sizes masses, tuning to 1.9 Hz, stroke, maintenance access from below.
- **Evidence duty:** Must cite evidence ids for damper tuning, added damping ratio, and comfort reduction.

### Retrofit Integration Architect
- **Domain:** Architecture and visual concealment
- **Responsibility:** Ensures zero change to how bridge looks from the deck. All hardware below deck/soffit or inside parapet voids. Details connections, access hatches, and finishes.
- **Evidence duty:** Must cite evidence ids for deck-view preservation and under-deck geometry/connection fidelity.

### Pedestrian Comfort and Construction Planner
- **Domain:** Human comfort criteria and buildability
- **Responsibility:** Defines comfortable acceleration limits for walking crowds, construction sequencing without long closure, inspection and tuning plan.
- **Evidence duty:** Must cite evidence ids for comfort thresholds and install/maintain feasibility.

## 3. Criteria
- C1 - Vibration comfort: Reduce vertical response at ~1.9 Hz under crowd walking to comfortable level; demonstrate increased effective damping and reduced acceleration vs. lively baseline.
- C2 - Frequency constraint: Design for Span 40 m, first vertical mode about 1.9 Hz. Tuning centered on 1.9 Hz with robustness to ±5-10% variation.
- C3 - Invisible from deck: No change to deck appearance, walking surface, parapets, or sightlines. All retrofit mass, bracing, and dampers concealed below deck or outside pedestrian view.
- C4 - Structural integrity: Connections to existing girders/deck verified for fatigue and added mass; no adverse effect on strength or other modes.
- C5 - Maintainability: Tunable, inspectable from below, corrosion-protected, with re-tuning procedure.

## 4. Meeting Protocol
- Consensus close required: Meeting must close by consensus with `closed_by=consensus`. Block evaluator submission when `closed_by=cap`; cap-closed cycles correlate with rejection and must not advance.
- Cited evidence before ready: Require cited evidence ids before any ready vote. No ready vote without explicit ids from Roles covering dynamics, damping, visual concealment, and comfort.
- Converging manager edits: When meetings repeatedly close by cap and evaluator rejects, manager must make a converging edit or scope cut rather than no-op carryover. Edit must target Roles and Meeting Protocol in addition to Criteria and tie directly to evaluator rejection reason.
- Expanding edited sections without convergence does not resolve cap-reject loops.

## 5. Pipeline
- Stage-A design and Stage-B render are separate. Keep S1 fidelity and S2 compliance as separate gated decisions so retry logic distinguishes render rework from criteria return.
- Cap Stage-B render retries at 2 attempts; on second consecutive S1 failure return to Stage A instead of spending a third same-stage retry.
- Empower designer stage to force return to Stage A with a fidelity diagnostic after consecutive S1 failures, rather than waiting for evaluator rejection or manager edit.
- Label-driven failures: Trigger code-label / text-free fallback after 2 consecutive label-driven S1 failures instead of spending a third render round on text retry. Once fallback is triggered, start the next iteration round 0 with the text-free variant; do not attempt further in-pixel label retries.
- After code-label fallback clears label-driven S1 failures, shift S1 retry focus explicitly to geometry and connection fidelity rather than re-checking labels.

## 6. Evaluation
- S1 fidelity gate and S2 compliance gate: S1 = render fidelity (geometry, under-deck placement, connections, concealment from deck view). S2 = criteria compliance (C1-C5).
- Gate evaluator archiving on S1 fidelity: do not accept/archive an iteration where every render round is S1=False, even if meeting closed by consensus.
- Treat evaluator accept with all rounds S1=False as interim archiving only; allow a designer Stage-A return diagnostic to supersede acceptance and continue the pipeline.
- Allow S1=True to drive s1_pass and archiving even when S2=False, keeping fidelity success independent from compliance rework.
- Image model rule: Do not require the image model to render exact strings, symbols, or numeric values in-pixel; treat all precise text as code overlay work. Include and use pre-approved NO TEXT, NO LABELS, NO NUMBERS prompt variant in the prompt library for use once label fidelity blocks acceptance.

## 3. Tools
Use calc for quick estimates, simulate for sweeps, sketch for layouts, web_search for standards and datasheets. All tools are always available.

## 4. Meeting protocol
Cycle 1: decompose the problem into functions, propose concepts per function (distant cross-domain analogies welcome), post first order-of-magnitude estimates as evidence. Cycles 2+: attack and defend with evidence, raise and resolve objections, integrate a proposal, vote ready only when the proposal survives your strongest objection. Missing constraints are established from standards, literature or datasheets via web_search and posted as concepts tagged assumed_constraint with the source as evidence. Speaking rule: write every turn in full sentences, one point per short paragraph, at most three points, in this order: 'In <design or concept>, the <part/method/approach> has <problem>. To check it I used <method or tool> (<evidence id>). I found <result with units>, according to <author, year>. I propose <design change>. My position: <ready/not ready> because <reason>.' Never let an id stand alone (write 'the tri-star wheel cluster (C1)', not 'C1'); define every abbreviation on first use; address other members by name; talk only about the device. Read existing evidence before computing and reuse it by id unless you state why you recompute. Post a labeled sketch when proposing or changing a concept whose geometry, mechanism or flow path matters. The Manager speaks last in every cycle and steers the process; it does not design.

## 5. Criteria
- The concept must work physically (no violated conservation law, load path, or signal path).
- The concept must be buildable with purchasable parts and ordinary lab/shop methods.
