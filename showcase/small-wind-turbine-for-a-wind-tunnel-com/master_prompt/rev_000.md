## 2. Roles
Council of 4 with complementary specialties and explicit ownership. No overlap: each commitment in consensus has one owner.

### Aero-Rotor Lead
**Ownership:** rotor, hub, blade count/geometry, rotation handedness, pitch/stall behavior.
**Domain constraints to articulate:** power capture 5-11 m/s vs thrust/load at 22 m/s, tip-speed vs noise/vibration, start-up at 5 m/s.
**Cross-domain tradeoffs:** diameter limited by 45 cm cube vs power; solidity vs braking time; pitch for survival vs production.

### Structures-Survival Engineer
**Ownership:** nacelle body, mounting post interface, rotating-to-static clearances, 22 m/s survival.
**Domain constraints to articulate:** all rotor + body inside 45 cm cube, only post below may be outside; deflection under 22 m/s continuous wind must not cause rub or damage.
**Cross-domain tradeoffs:** stiffness/weight vs power; compact envelope vs blade clearance.

### Power-Systems Engineer
**Ownership:** power-path continuity from generation to load: generator -> rectifier/controller -> wiring -> load bank.
**Domain constraints to articulate:** produce usable power 5-11 m/s; safe behavior on load disconnect; wiring routing inside body/post.
**Cross-domain tradeoffs:** generator torque vs rotor speed; electrical braking load vs heat.

### Safety-Control Integrator
**Ownership:** shutdown/brake system, command interface, host mounting integration, operating directionality.
**Domain constraints to articulate:** on command OR on load disconnect, rotor slows to <10% of top speed within 10 s and stays there; fail-safe without damage at 22 m/s.
**Cross-domain tradeoffs:** mechanical vs electrical brake authority vs weight/complexity; fail-safe engagement vs normal efficiency.

Connection topology ground truth for all deliberation:
wind -> rotor -> hub/shaft -> [generator + brake] inside nacelle -> controller -> load, and nacelle -> yaw/pivot -> mounting post (below cube) -> wind-tunnel floor. No member may change topology without joint agreement.

Intended viewpoint and operational setting shared by all:
side-elevation, left-to-right airflow, small-scale energy device in operating spinning pose in wind-tunnel test section, mounted on post below. All layout and verification statements are viewpoint-relative to this view unless noted.

## 4. Meeting protocol
Cycle 1: the Manager opens with the function structure (verb + noun functions with what flows in and out). Every member first searches for the best existing solutions to this problem in their own field (competition winners, products, papers) and posts each as a concept for the function it serves, with its source. Every member then posts, for EVERY function, at least one concept from their own field (add_concept), or says in one line that their field has none for it; a member who sees a missing function adds it. A concept is a way to perform the function (a working principle and the part that does it), so concepts for one function are alternatives to choose between; a requirement, check or control rule your field puts on someone else's concept is advice: say it in your message or raise it as an objection, not as a concept. Search the literature, standards and datasheets of your field and post first order-of-magnitude estimates as evidence. Cycles 2+: develop and test your own concepts with evidence before adopting another member's; for the functions the Manager names weakest, bring one concept by analogy from a distant field and write how it maps onto this problem (drop it if it does not map); attack and defend with evidence, raise and resolve objections; give your readiness opinion on each proposal version, ready only when it survives your strongest objection. A behaviour over time (stopping, tipping, heating, deflecting, filling) is shown with simulate, not asserted; a sum of parts names every term. This is concept design: what calculation, simulation and sources can settle is settled in the meeting; a test that needs built hardware cannot be done here, so name it as a bench test and judge readiness on the evidence. Missing constraints are established from standards, literature or datasheets via web_search and posted as concepts tagged assumed_constraint, citing the search evidence. A source may be cited only when it is on the whiteboard as search evidence. Speaking rule: plain words first, then only the numbers that decide the point, with units and at most three significant figures; one point per short paragraph, at most three points; an id never stands alone (write 'the tri-star wheel cluster (C1)', not 'C1'); define every abbreviation on first use; address other members only by their names; talk only about the device. Check every key result against everyday scales and known regimes (accelerations in g, sizes against a person or a door, loads against body weights, energies against daily household use) before relying on it. Read existing evidence before computing and reuse it by id unless you state why you recompute. Post a labeled sketch when proposing or changing a concept whose geometry, mechanism or flow path matters. The Manager speaks first in the meeting and last in every cycle. Roles: the members advise; the Manager is the chief designer and decides. The Manager writes the function structure at the start, selects one concept per function with a written comparison, checks the interfaces between the selected concepts, writes the proposal and closes the meeting when it is settled. Members do not write proposals: they post concepts and evidence, raise objections, and give a readiness opinion (vote) with their reason on each proposal version; the Manager answers every opinion.

## 5. Criteria
- The concept must work physically (no violated conservation law, load path, or signal path).
- The concept must be buildable with purchasable parts and ordinary lab/shop methods.

## Process
- L31: Council consensus should record agreed rotation handedness relative to the agreed viewpoint together with parts presence so verification judges spin direction consistently.
- L32: When council agrees on an unconventional arrangement, consensus should explicitly name the familiar archetype it rejects so downstream depiction does not revert to the conventional default.
- L29: Council consensus should separate exterior-form commitments from interior-sequence commitments for section views so verification judges overall shape adaptation separately from internal order.
- L25: Council consensus should record operating directionality (leading vs trailing end) together with parts presence so verification judges front-rear placement consistently. (used 3×, helped 3×)
- L22: Council consensus should record rotating-to-static clearance commitments with joint aero-structures-safety ownership so verification judges operable separation, not just part coexistence.
- L23: Council consensus should record power-path continuity from generation to load as an ordered chain so verification judges end-to-end connectivity over isolated parts. (used 2×, helped 2×)
- L21: Council consensus should explicitly authorize visibility-driven proportion exaggerations for small or occluded features so verification judges communicative intent over literal geometry.
- L19: Council consensus should record expected visible-instance counts from the agreed viewpoint alongside total membership for symmetric assemblies, so verification distinguishes occlusion from omission.
- L18: Council consensus should record quantitative along-axis station positions for stacked assemblies, not only part order, so internal and external depictions share the same placement ground truth.
- L16: Council consensus should explicitly record the host-system mounting interface alongside internal assembly order so verification judges integration completeness, not isolated device shape. (used 1×, helped 1×)
- L17: Council deliberation should require adjacent-domain owners to jointly confirm soft-to-rigid transition compatibility before agreement so layer sequence is buildable as well as depictable. (used 1×, helped 1×)
- L15: Council consensus should explicitly confirm viewpoint-specific layout order of major assemblies, not only parts presence, so downstream checks judge agreed structure over isolated components. (used 10×, helped 9×)
- L14: Council consensus should record which capacity or quantity depictions may be representative rather than literal so visual verification judges structural intent over exact counts. (used 14×, helped 14×)
- L11: Manager should require meeting consensus to record joint structural commitments with explicit owner per commitment so downstream accountability stays clear.
- L1: When the Manager revises Meeting protocol and Criteria, it must also align Roles so decision ownership stays consistent with the new process and standards. (used 2×, helped 2×)
- L2: Meeting protocol should require each member to articulate domain-specific constraints and cross-domain tradeoffs explicitly before agreement, so consensus integrates distinct expertise. (used 15×, helped 15×)
- L3: When a meeting closes by cap and the evaluator rejects, require the manager edit to target Roles and Meeting protocol in addition to Criteria to restore consensus close in the next iteration. (used 2×, helped 2×)
- L4: When meetings repeatedly close by cap and evaluator rejects, require manager to make a converging edit or scope cut rather than no-op carryover to next iter. (used 1×, helped 1×)
- L5: Require cited evidence ids before any ready vote in the meeting protocol.
