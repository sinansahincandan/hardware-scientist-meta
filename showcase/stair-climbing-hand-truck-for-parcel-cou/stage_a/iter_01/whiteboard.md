## Inspirations
- A1: Overall judgment: the tri-star walking principle with a twin-tube backbone and a spring-applied lock is physically sound and buildable from catalogue parts, and it is a real step beyond the closest fixed-frame 60 kg stair truck, but I would not release this to detail design yet because the folded length cannot contain its own backbone and the braced-stiffness claim uses the wrong structural model, and the stair torque and mass numbers are used inconsistently. The biggest worry is that the 1100 mm one-piece backbone is claimed to stow in a 730 mm suitcase while two 370 mm tri-star clusters are claimed to fit in 380 by 190 mm, which does not add up, followed by the claim that a transverse clip-on brace cuts cantilever deflection by eight times when it does not provide a ground support. Compared with the closest prior art, a steel tri-star sack truck rated 150 kg flat and 60 kg on stairs with 150 mm wheels and skids, and the UpCart tri-wheel folder and WO/2023/137072 auto-brake patent, the concept keeps the proven tri-star class and adds a folding van stow plus a dead-man disc plus positive tooth lock for hands-off holding that the fixed skid trucks do not have. That novelty is credible, but the analysis must be corrected first.

Decisive numbers and formulas: parcel weight follows W = m*g, and 60 kg gives about 589 N (E7), which is correct. Toe root moment follows M = W*a, and 589 N at 0.15 m gives 88.3 N m (E68), which is correct arithmetic, but the backbone stress then uses 589 N at 0.20 m shared by two tubes (E70). The formula sigma = M*y/I is the right bending regime for a tube, and tube inertia I = pi*(D^4-d^4)/64 for 30 by 26 mm gives 1.73e-08 m4 (E69), which I recompute as correct, and per-tube moment 58.9 N m times 0.015 m divided by that I gives about 51.1 MPa (E70), which I recompute as correct for the 0.20 m input. The inconsistency matters for traceability: the design uses 0.15 m in one place and 0.20 m in another for the same parcel offset, so the room must pick one parcel centre and reuse it, and the static factor 276/51.1 = 5.4 (E74) is correct for the higher moment but the claimed 3.60 with impact has no calculation on the board. Tip deflection follows delta = F*L^3/(3*E*I) for a cantilever, and half weight 294.5 N on 1.1 m with E about 68.9 GPa and the above I gives about 0.11 m (E71), which I recompute as correct. The braced claim of 13.8 mm multiplies by 0.5 cubed (E85), which is the formula for halving the cantilever span to a new ground support, but a clip across the two tubes does not ground the midspan in fore-aft bending where both tubes already bend together, so the model is outside its regime and the corrected deployed deflection remains near 110 mm unless the brace is re-designed as a stayed support, which matters because 110 mm whip changes handling and cluster alignment on the 197 mm riser. Axle torsion follows tau = 16*T/(pi*d^3), and 33.5 N m on 20 mm diameter gives about 21.3 MPa (E88), which I recompute as correct and safe, and toe-pin single shear follows tau = F/A with 294.5 N on an 8 mm pin giving about 5.86 MPa (E89), which I recompute as correct and safe. Lift energy follows E = m*g*h, and 60 kg through 0.197 m gives about 116 J (E22), which is correct arithmetic but uses parcel only while the operator lifts 66.7 kg total for about 129 J, so the board understates climb work by about ten percent. The stair simulation reports pivot torque about 38.8 N m per side with 188 N descent hold and 24 N flat push (E23) and separately 67.0 N m locked torque from slope times 0.185 m radius (E51), and my check with total weight 652 N on stair angle arctan(0.197/0.254) = 37.8 deg gives slope about 400 N and total torque about 66.6 N m, so 67.0 N m total is plausible but 39.0 N m per side would be 78 N m total and does not agree with 67.0 N m, and the room must settle total versus per-side use because the caliper clamp and pin are sized from it.

Self-consistency: parts, connections and sequence largely agree that the toe-plate hinges at the low-moment root to the backbone, the backbone carries bending to the main axle, the axle carries two tri-star clusters, and the cable runs with a slack loop from the grips to the caliper and tooth pin (E72, E52, E87), which is a coherent load path and signal path with no violated conservation law. The contradiction is geometric: the backbone is defined as two 1100 mm one-piece tubes (fabrication step 1) with a handle that slides inside them, yet the folded envelope is claimed as 730 by 380 by 190 mm for 52.7 L (E42) with 390 mm door clearance (E76). A 1100 mm bar cannot stow in 730 mm length, and a 370 mm overall cluster diameter from 110 mm centre radius plus 75 mm wheel radius cannot be inferred to fit in 190 mm thickness without a stacked-dimension sketch, and the toe-plate 365 mm width plus clusters outside the frame are not summed across the 380 mm width. A limit argued only by a sketch annotation without summing the parts it names is not shown. Worst case is partly addressed: the lock is sized for the steepest IRC stair from 197 mm riser and 254 mm going (E16, E24) and the van restraint uses 0.8 times tare weight for 51 N (E67), which is the right holding-force idea, but sustained hand limits are exceeded at 209 N pull and 188 N hold with a ratio 188/130 = 1.45 (E59), so the safety case rests entirely on the spring-applied lock holding the largest slope load of about 400 N while released, not on the dynamic pull number, and that holding margin with impact and cold, tired hands needs the lever-force calc above. Human-factor limits on the board rest on search results whose pages were never opened (E77, E78, E79), which is weaker than values read from opened pages like the van space (E36) and stair code (E16), so the room should replace them with opened sources in the requested calc.

Functions, flows and meeting facts: support has the backbone, restraint has the strap, flat motion has rolling on the cluster wheels at 24 N, stair traverse has cluster pivoting, limiting has disc plus tooth pin, and collapse has folding toe plus sliding handle plus removable brace, so no function is missing and the chief designer gave a means for each. Material flow from parcel to toe-plate to backbone to axle to clusters to step is continuous and the hand-lever to cable to caliper to plate chain meets where the layout places it, with the slack loop correctly preventing fold-induced release. There are no open objections and all four members marked ready on v2, with the tired-hand lever and tooth engagement named as remaining work; that work is test work the concept review must not depend on for acceptance, and I have rephrased it above as computations the room can do. The concept is buildable with purchasable tube, plate, solid-rubber wheels, 160 mm rotor and pin using TIG welding and bolting, and the cost class is plausible, but the room should not accept on hope that the suitcase fits or that the brace grounds the beam. Fix the three computations and the concept can go forward.

## Chief designer's selections
- F2 -> C23: Restrain needs preload to backrest. The webbing strap (C23) is better because it is the only working means preventing shift and shock doubling; the visibility rule (C10) is same as needed sightline limit but does no restraining, and empty fields (C5, C18) are worse as they offer no means.
- F3 -> C2: Flat motion needs rolling. The paired small wheels rolling as fixed cart (C2) is better because it is the only working rolling means giving 24.0 N push (E23); the loop grips (C8) are same as needed push interface but do no rolling, and empty fields (C16, C25) are worse as they offer no means.
- F4 -> C1: Stair traverse needs step walking. The tri-star cluster (C1) is better because it is the only working means pivoting over the 197 mm riser with 185 mm radius and 39.0 N m per side (E23) costing 116 J per riser (E22); pull posture (C11) is same as needed operating method but does no stepping, fold-down handle note (C19) is worse as it only aids stow, and empty fields (C6, C15, C26) offer no means.
- F5 -> C7: Limiting needs auto-lock with no back-drive. The dead-man lever with disc plus tooth park pin (C7 combined with C20 and C3 detail) is better because release means locked holding 67.0 N m (E51) against 209 N pull and 188 N hold (E23) exceeding 130-140 N sustained limits; friction-only contact brake (C3) alone is worse as it cannot be trusted without positive lock, and empty fields (C17, C27) offer no means.
- F6 -> C13: Collapse still needs telescoping handle plus folding toe-plate (C13) for 730 by 380 by 190 mm stow, now with clip-on transverse brace (C28) that pins across twin tubes when deployed and unclips alongside for fold, because braced deflection 13.8 mm versus 0.11 m whip keeps backbone one piece with joint count at toe plus handle plus one removable brace.
- F1 -> C22: Keep twin-tube backbone C22 upsized to 40 by 36 mm over triangulated stay C29 because transverse brace cannot ground cantilever tip per E93 and E115, upsize cuts whip to 43.9 mm per E116 with 40.9 MPa stress per E118 for 0.37 kg penalty per E117, while stay adds joint in peak 118 Nm path per E90 with 503 N tie per E119.
