Lunar regolith excavation robot

Future lunar bases need loose surface soil (regolith) for building and for extracting oxygen. Design a small robot that drives over a bed of lunar soil simulant, digs it up, carries it and dumps it into a collection bin, operated remotely or autonomously by a team that cannot see or hear the robot.

  • The whole robot, including its battery, weighs at most 60 kg (the soil it carries is not counted)
  • At the start the whole robot fits inside a box 1.0 m long, 0.5 m wide and 0.5 m high; it may unfold after the start
  • Digs Black Point 1 (BP-1) lunar basalt regolith simulant
  • Controlled remotely or autonomously, with the operators out of sight and hearing of the robot
Judge: Hardware Scientist is best. C is the only design that shows all three box dimensions and the mass with sums that add up, and its deciding numbers (cut force vs grip, fill time, stop distance, sinkage) re-derive correctly. Its mechanism has a part for every step. B's bucket-ladder concept is sound but its rate and power numbers are wrong as written and its picture is unlabelled. A fails the stowed length, under-counts its mass, and has no way to lift soil from the blade into the hopper. (In the judge's words: A = Hardware Scientist, open weights, B = Single prompt, C = Hardware Scientist.)

Tactile fingertip sensor for robot hands

Robot hands drop and crush objects because they cannot feel what they hold. Design a fingertip for a robot hand that feels touch the way a human fingertip does: where it is touched, how hard, and when an object starts to slip.

  • Shaped and sized like a human fingertip, mountable on a robot finger
  • Detects contact forces as small as about 1 mN
Judge: Hardware Scientist is best. B makes both constraints checkable: the 15 mm width is summed from its layers, and the 1 mN signal (443 fF baseline, 5.54 fF change) re-derives correctly, with a cutaway picture that broadly matches the layer stack. C has a comparable capacitive chain and the best making detail, but its numbers have small errors and its picture is mislabelled and wrongly scaled. A uses a proven vision principle but does not show that 1 mN is resolvable, and its picture has the camera pointing the wrong way. C and A tie on total; C is placed second because its 1 mN chain is quantified end to end. (In the judge's words: A = Single prompt, B = Hardware Scientist, C = Hardware Scientist, open weights.)

Small wind turbine for a wind-tunnel competition

Design a small wind turbine that produces as much electricity as possible in a wind tunnel, survives strong gusts, and shuts itself down safely on command or when its electrical load is disconnected.

  • Every part of the turbine (rotor and body) fits inside a 45 cm cube; only the mounting post below it may be outside
  • Survives continuous wind of 22 m/s without damage
  • Produces power between 5 and 11 m/s wind
  • When the load is disconnected or on command, the rotor slows to below 10% of its top speed within 10 seconds and stays there
Judge: Hardware Scientist is best. A ties every constraint to a number that re-derives (power chain, post stress and deflection, phase-short current and stop time) and has a hardware path for 'slows and stays parked'. Its labelled picture shows the right three-blade rotor at a sensible scale. C has a sound feathering concept and the best making plan, but its stop time and survival margin are asserted and its picture has four blades. B has no constraint check and relies on feathering it never provides hardware for. (In the judge's words: A = Hardware Scientist, B = Hardware Scientist, open weights, C = Single prompt.)
