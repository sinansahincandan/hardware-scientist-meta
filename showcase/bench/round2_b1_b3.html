Lunar regolith excavation robot

Future lunar bases need loose surface soil (regolith) for building and for extracting oxygen. Design a small robot that drives over a bed of lunar soil simulant, digs it up, carries it and dumps it into a collection bin, operated remotely or autonomously by a team that cannot see or hear the robot.

  • The whole robot, including its battery, weighs at most 60 kg (the soil it carries is not counted)
  • At the start the whole robot fits inside a box 1.0 m long, 0.5 m wide and 0.5 m high; it may unfold after the start
  • Digs Black Point 1 (BP-1) lunar basalt regolith simulant
  • Controlled remotely or autonomously, with the operators out of sight and hearing of the robot
Judge: Single prompt is best. C and A tie on total, but C has the more credible deciding numbers for a buildable machine: a mass budget that covers every subsystem, a sized battery and a proven bucket-ladder concept with frank risks, whereas A's 24.4 kg tare visibly omits major parts. A's picture and arithmetic are cleaner, which is why it is close; B trails because its picture puts the operator laptop on the robot and its force basis is unexplained. (In the judge's words: A = Hardware Scientist, C = Single prompt.)

Tactile fingertip sensor for robot hands

Robot hands drop and crush objects because they cannot feel what they hold. Design a fingertip for a robot hand that feels touch the way a human fingertip does: where it is touched, how hard, and when an object starts to slip.

  • Shaped and sized like a human fingertip, mountable on a robot finger
  • Detects contact forces as small as about 1 mN
Judge: Hardware Scientist is best. B is the only design that carries the 1 mN requirement through to a signal it can check (2.08 um dent to 0.882 fF on 212 fF), with every step re-deriving correctly and its remaining doubt stated plainly. Its picture is labelled, matches the text and is at human-fingertip scale. C has the same architecture but never computes the 1 mN signal and its picture is about 1.5 times oversize; A's key 1 mN argument confuses contact pressure with fluid pressure. (In the judge's words: A = Single prompt, B = Hardware Scientist.)

Small wind turbine for a wind-tunnel competition

Design a small wind turbine that produces as much electricity as possible in a wind tunnel, survives strong gusts, and shuts itself down safely on command or when its electrical load is disconnected.

  • Every part of the turbine (rotor and body) fits inside a 45 cm cube; only the mounting post below it may be outside
  • Survives continuous wind of 22 m/s without damage
  • Produces power between 5 and 11 m/s wind
  • When the load is disconnected or on command, the rotor slows to below 10% of its top speed within 10 seconds and stays there
Judge: Single prompt is best. C is the only design whose shutdown and gust survival do not depend on the brake winning against a still-driving rotor: spring-return feathering removes the aerodynamic torque, so 'below 10% and stays there' holds even unpowered, and its numbers and risks are otherwise sound. B has the most complete brake arithmetic and ranks ahead of A, which left the stop time uncalculated; B's and C's pictures are weak (B about 68 cm long against a 45 cm cube, C with four blades). (In the judge's words: A = Hardware Scientist, C = Single prompt.)
