## Inspirations
- A1: Overall judgment: the working principle holds sunlight to electricity to batteries to vapour-compression cold air over crates near 5C is physically sound and matches the closest prior art, a 1.5 tonne Nakuru County unit with 2 kW array plus 10 kWh lithium-iron-phosphate bank holding 4 to 6C and running 21 hours per day autonomously, but this proposal would not go to detail design yet because the solar balance closes with only 0.3 kWh per day margin on inconsistent loads, with no battery autonomy sizing and no COP conversion, and it depends on staged 250 kg per day discipline. My biggest worry is cloudy-day and full-harvest performance at 43C ambient. Compared with prior art it is a narrower 1-tonne staged 3.6 kWp split-array PIR-box instantiation, not a new principle.

Formulas and recomputation: conduction as Q equals U times A times dT with U about 0.21 W per m2 K for 100 to 120 mm polyisocyanurate, area 25.4 m2, dT 38 K gives 203 W (E41), and arithmetic checks as 0.21 times 25.4 times 38 equals 202.7 W. Interior volume as length times width times height gives 8.64 m3 (E39) and envelope as 2 times sum of face areas gives 25.4 m2 (E40), both correct. Door infiltration as air changes times volume times volumetric heat capacity times dT gives 4 times 8.64 times 0.335 times 38 divided by 1000 equals 0.44 kWh per day (E57), correct for sensible air only. Staged pull-down as mass times specific heat times dT divided by 3600 gives 250 times 3.6 times 25 divided by 3600 equals 6.25 kWh (E28) and full-tonne gives 25 kWh (E26), both correct. Array yield as peak kW times peak sun hours times derate gives 3.6 times 5.0 times 0.75 equals 13.5 kWh per day (E83) and required peak as load divided by peak sun hours times derate gives 13.2 divided by 3.75 equals 3.52 kWp (E82), arithmetic correct. Margin as 13.5 minus 13.2 equals 0.3 kWh per day (E86) is correct arithmetically but the 13.2 kWh per day load (E58) uses 203 plus 50 plus 40 times 24 divided by 1000 plus 6.2 and omits the 0.44 kWh per day door term, so the consistent total is about 13.7 kWh per day and exceeds yield. The staged-average 615 W claim (E28) implies 14.8 kWh per day as 615 times 24 divided by 1000, which contradicts 13.2 kWh per day and leaves the balance unsettled. The comparison also equates thermal watts to electrical yield without dividing by COP, which is the wrong model regime for vapour compression and must be corrected, though the error is conservative because COP above 1 would increase margin. Pad pressure as mass times gravity divided by pads divided by pad area gives about 18.8 kPa for grown mass (E89), recomputed as 1841.5 times 9.81 divided by 6 divided by 0.16 equals 18.8 kPa, correct and well below 150 kPa. Nine-module footprint as 9 times 1.722 times 1.134 equals 17.6 m2 (E84) is correct and cannot fit the 4.32 m2 roof, so the split 2 plus 7 layout is needed. Wind pressure as 0.5 times density times velocity squared gives 375 Pa at 25 m per s (E88) and uplift mass equivalent as area times pressure divided by gravity gives 17.6 times 375 divided by 9.81 equals 673 kg (E90), both correct and about the weight of nine adults, so anchoring matters.

Self agreement: part counts agree as 9 modules, 50 crates at 20 kg for 1000 kg (E49), 12.5 crates per day for 250 kg (E78), and 4 days to fill. Connections agree as array charges battery via controller, battery powers cooler, cooler blows air into box that encloses crates and stands on foundation, control switches cooler. Operating sequence follows the same chain. Sizes broadly agree. The single foundation entry lumps roof gantry plus ground frame, which is acceptable as a system but should be drawn as two structures. No part is in two places and no moving path crosses another.

Worst case: full 1000 kg loaded warm at once needs 25 kWh pull-down plus about 7.5 kWh steady thermal in one day, about 32.5 kWh thermal, which cannot be met by 13.5 kWh per day electrical in one day even at COP 2, so the design only works with the stated 250 kg per day rule, 8 daytime openings with strip curtain and single-crate door discipline. With 2% margin any cloud, extra openings, 43C-plus condenser derate, or dust derate breaks autonomy. Night load must be carried by batteries but usable kilowatt-hours, depth of discharge, surge, and one-cloudy-day autonomy are not sized. Wind uplift while holding temperature must be resisted by screw anchors whose capacity is not shown.

Four review checks: (0) every function has a means with convert by split array, store by battery bank, remove heat by monoblock, enclose by sandwich box, hold by racking plus crates, regulate by thermostat plus probes, so no missing function. (1) mass across pads sums produce plus crates plus racking plus panels plus door plus battery plus eutectic plus misc as 1780 kg (E66) plus 3 modules to 1840 kg (E87), but the sum omits tube frames, monoblock, battery box, and foundation self-weight, so it does not show the limit even though margin to 150 kPa is factor 8 by everyday scale of a person standing at about 20 to 30 kPa. (1b) numeric limits are computed for holding and yield, but the energy limit is unknown until COP conversion and consistent door inclusion and battery kilowatt-hours are computed. (2) two values exist for the same quantity and must be resolved as full-tonne heat 25000 kWh from a divide-by-3.6 error (E50) versus correct 25 kWh (E26) with the design using the latter, envelope 24 m2 in the simulation (E28) versus 25.4 m2 used for conduction (E40) with the latter controlling, and door as 0.44 kWh per day sensible (E57) versus 70 W average or 1.68 kWh per day in the simulation (E28) with the design claiming the smaller value while the larger erases margin. (3) energy flow from sun to array to battery to cooler to cold air to crates, and signal flow from return-air plus produce probe to thermostat to monoblock with battery shed and attendant log, are each owned by a part and meet at stated interfaces, with no gap except cooler penetration and drain detail left to detail design. (4) there is no open blocking objection and all members called ready, with the chief designer settling split array, battery plus thermal buffer, monoblock, PIR box, racking, and probe control, so the meeting facts leave the concept settled in principle but the analysis gaps above prevent release.

Deviations and scales: 1000 kg in 8.64 m3 is like a small bedroom holding fourteen adults by weight, plausible. 13.5 kWh per day is about one-and-a-half households and 615 W average is about half a microwave running continuously, plausible for a small cold room. Literature support is mixed because the viability paper and ColdHubs plus FAO plus door guidance were opened, while U-value, panel weight, soil capacity, and many insolation and battery pages were search-results only and are weaker where they decide acceptance. Buildability with purchasable 400 W modules, cam-lock PIR, 48 V lithium-iron-phosphate, vented crates, and shop-built tube frames plus precast pads is credible with ordinary lab and shop methods. Remaining bench testing of 250 kg daytime pull-down timing at 43C ambient belongs in feedback after the requested energy, battery, mass, layout, and COP calculations are closed.
