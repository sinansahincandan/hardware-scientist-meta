# Hardware Scientist — 3-minute talk (about 20 seconds per slide, ~440 words)

**1 · From a one-paragraph problem to a concept that works.**
Early concept design decides most of what a device costs and whether it fails. One AI prompt gives a confident
picture: no sources, no calculations, no check. Hardware Scientist is an AI design team that teaches itself. This is
the whole input for an infant warmer; this came out: wax pouches charged in hot water keep the baby at 37 degrees for
six hours, for about fifty dollars.

**2 · Four loops.**
Experts work on a shared whiteboard and a chief designer decides. An Evaluator checks every number; a rejection
rewrites the exact prompt section at fault, and the next meeting continues on the same board. The picture is rendered,
segmented with SAM and criticised until it matches. Lessons and skills carry to the next problem.

**3 · The whiteboard.**
The braille display meeting. Cycle one: four experts post 21 concepts, starting from the best existing products, with
54 sources. Cycle four: one chosen concept per function, fifteen calculations, a sketch, and every expert ready.

**4 · A rejection that changed the design.**
The Evaluator asked, among four things: calculate the magnet latch force. The Manager rewrote only the prompt sections
at fault. On the same board the council found the ferrite sheet holds 0.03 newtons against a finger's 0.3. It could
never work. Magnets at 1.6 newtons were accepted, at 2.27 dollars a cell.

**5 · The picture is checked, not trusted.**
The first render looks fine, but the critic measures it: 2.7 times too big, heating mat hidden. One targeted redraw
later, SAM finds all eight parts where the design puts them. Only then come labels and scale.

**6 · Results.**
Five public problems — NASA Lunabotics, a robot fingertip, the DOE wind competition, an infant warmer, a braille
display — five accepted concepts, drawn in use with scale, assembly steps and a measurement chain. Where the
drawing still differs from the design, as with the braille magnets, the critic says so and it is not called final.

**7 · Against one prompt.**
Same Meta models, one prompt: 70 seconds, a picture that contradicts its own text, numbers "from a CAD" that does not
exist. Hardware Scientist compares up to 30 concepts, cites up to 26 sources, runs about 20 calculations and computes
every limit. A blind judge puts it level or ahead; the difference is engineering an engineer can trace.

**8 · Open weights.**
Swap one client file and the same system runs on Meta's open-weights Muse Spark Open and SAM 3: the same designs
accepted, same cost, half the time.

**9 · Does it work?**
The slide that matters. For every concept the deciding limit is computed: six hours against four, 1.6 newtons against
0.3, a one-millinewton touch at 44 times the noise. What only hardware can prove is named as a bench test, not assumed.
Concepts that work, from one paragraph.
