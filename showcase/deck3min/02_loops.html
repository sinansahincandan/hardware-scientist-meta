How it works

Four loops that close on themselves

1 Council on a whiteboard

Four domain experts post concepts for every function, each backed by sources they opened, calculations, simulations and sketches. A chief designer picks one concept per function and writes the proposal; the experts give reasoned readiness opinions.

Makes: 20–30 concepts compared, an evidence-backed proposal

2 Evaluator → TextGrad

The Evaluator sees only the board, re-derives the numbers and checks every limit. A rejection becomes written gradients that point at the prompt section that caused it; the Manager edits that section, the next meeting continues on the same board, and the same requests are re-checked first.

Makes: a revised master prompt, closed evidence requests

3 Picture loop

Muse Image renders the device photoreal; SAM outlines every part; a critic compares the pixels with the design (sizes, counts, connections) and sends a targeted redraw until the picture is faithful.

Makes: a labelled figure with scale, assembly steps and measurement chain

4 Knowledge across problems

After each run, lessons, reusable engineering skills and new tools are written down and handed to the next problem's council.

Makes: 28 lessons and 25 skills carried into a new run; 2–3 new skills from each

Every model call runs on Meta models: Muse Spark, Muse Image and SAM.
