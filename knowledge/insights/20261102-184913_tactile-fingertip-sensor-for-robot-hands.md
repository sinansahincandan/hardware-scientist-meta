# Insights from Tactile fingertip sensor for robot hands
Source run: 20261102-184913_tactile-fingertip-sensor-for-robot-hands

## Human fingertip acuity sets tactile array pitch
Human fingertip two-point discrimination is on the order 2-8 mm. A robot fingertip contact of ~15x20 mm (~300 mm2, consistent with 15-20 mm finger width anthropometry) sampled at ~2.5 mm pitch yields ~48 taxels and approximates human-like localization. This sets a general rule: human-grade localization needs 2-3 mm taxel pitch, i.e. 40-60 taxels per fingertip.
Evidence: E1 (search: human fingertip tactile spatial resolution two-point discrimination: en.wikipedia.org — Two-point discrimination — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-point_discrimination); E12 (search: human fingertip diameter size anthropometry: researchgate.net — Percentile values of hand dimensions (mm) in right and left hands. — https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Percentile-values-of-hand-dimensions-mm-in-right-and-left-hands_tbl2_286964087); E8 (pressure from 1mN on taxel and fingertip area estimate: fingertip area m2: 0.0003 meter ** 2; taxels: 48.0; C base pF: 0.111)

## Millinewton capacitive touch is a femtofarad signal regime
A 1 mN detection threshold on one 2.5 mm taxel (~6.25 mm2) is only ~160 Pa. A parallel-plate taxel of that footprint with ~1 mm gap has base capacitance ~0.1-0.33 pF. With solid silicone ~1 MPa the light-touch strain is ~1e-4 for sub-0.1 fF change, buried in parasitics. Reducing effective modulus by area-fraction structuring to ~0.02 MPa raises strain to ~0.008 and change to ~2.7 fF. Rule: mN capacitive touch lives in the few-fF delta regime and requires fF-resolution conversion, guarded multiplexing, and per-taxel zero/gain calibration.
Evidence: E16 (pressure of 1mN on one 2.5mm taxel: 0.16 mN/mm²); E35 (base capacitance pF for 2.5mm taxel: 0.332); E36 (strain at 1mN with soft 20kPa effective pillars: 0.008); E39 (effective pillar modulus from area fraction: 0.02 MPa)

## Slip vibration bandwidth and dual-rate telemetry rule
Human Pacinian-mediated slip/texture vibration extends to ~200-500 Hz. Nyquist requires >1000 Hz sampling for 500 Hz content; ~2000 Hz provides ~2x margin. A 48-taxel static array scanned row-column at ~25 Hz plus one fast vibration channel at 2000 Hz totals ~43 kbps (~19.2 kbps static + 24 kbps vibration), readily carried on a 4-wire serial bus. Rule: split slow quasi-static force at tens of Hz from fast slip acoustics at kHz.
Evidence: E19 (search: human Pacinian corpuscle vibration frequency slip detection 200 Hz tactile sensor: iiab.me — Mechanoreceptor — 2022 — https://iiab.me/kiwix/content/wikipedia_en_molcell_maxi_2022-11/A/Mechanoreceptor); E23 (data rate and Nyquist for 48 taxels plus vibration channel: static 19200 bps, vib 24000 bps, total 43.2 kbps; Nyquist for 500 Hz needs >1000 Hz sampling, choose 2000 Hz margin 2x); E18 (search: capacitive tactile sensor array multiplexed readout capacitance to digital AD7147: ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org — A Flexible and Robust Large Scale Capacitive Tactile System for Robots — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1411.6837)

## Gripper fingertip pressure and soft-skin strain regime
Holding a ~1 kg object needs ~15 N normal per finger assuming friction; nominal robot grip design is ~30 N per fingertip. Over ~300 mm2 this is ~100 kPa nominal with local peaks to ~460 kPa from curvature and dynamics. Shore A 30 silicone with modulus ~1 MPa reaches ~0.46 compressive strain at peaks, near densification, so fingertip elastomers must bottom out or carry overload in rigid structure. Complete fingertip-scale stacks of this type are ~5 g, negligible for grip payload but relevant for finger inertia.
Evidence: E32 (mount load vs grip and drop check: area_m2 0.0003 p_max_kPa 463.3 p_grip_kPa 100.0 F_needed_N 14.8); E34 (skin compressive strain at worst-case pressure assuming 1 MPa modulus for Shore A 30 silicone: 0.463); E38 (mass sum fingertip cartridge for mount payload: 4.89 g)
