# Insights from Lunar regolith excavation robot
Source run: 20261103-152604_lunar-regolith-excavation-robot

## Lunar simulant bulk density and shear regime
BP-1 lunar regolith simulant has bulk density around 1.5-1.6 kg per L, 20 L masses about 31 kg, with low cohesion and high friction angle phi around 38-42 deg. Limiting traction on loose simulant follows W tan phi, coefficient 0.78-0.90, for example about 749 N thrust from 893 N normal load.
Evidence: E2 (search: BP-1 lunar simulant bulk density angle of repose: ares.jsc.nasa.gov — ARES | Exploration Science Projects | Lunar Regolith Simulants | BP-1 — https://ares.jsc.nasa.gov/projects/simulants/bp-1.html); E3 (search: BP-1 lunar simulant bulk density angle of repose: simulantdb.com — Planetary Simulant Database — https://www.simulantdb.com/simulants/bp1.html); E8 (hopper 20L soil mass: 31 kg); E11 (search: lunar regolith simulant BP-1 cohesion friction angle shear strength: impact.colorado.edu — | BEVAHIOR  OF BP-1 AND  LHS-1 LUNAR REGOLITH  SIMULANTS UNDER VACUUM  CONDITIONS. |  | — https://impact.colorado.edu/lungradcon/presentations/142140b1); E12 (search: lunar regolith simulant BP-1 cohesion friction angle shear strength: hou.usra.edu — Developing  a New  Space Economy (2019)  5068.pdf Lightning Talk or Poster — 2019 — https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lunarisru2019/pdf/5068.pdf); E27 (traction estimate W tan phi: 749 N)

## Small soil cutting specific energy
Shallow milling and trenching in loose soil exhibits specific cutting energy on the order of 0.45 J per cm3. Mechanical cutting power equals specific energy times volumetric rate, for example 150 cm3 per s from 0.30 m width x 25 mm depth x 0.02 m per s advance draws about 67.5 W, equivalent to about 67.5 N tangential force at 1 m per s tip speed.
Evidence: E13 (search: soil cutting specific energy bucket ladder trencher small: jstage.jst.go.jp — Studies on a Small Trencher (I) — https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsam1937/46/3/46_3_359/_article/-char/en); E15 (volume rate cm3/s for 0.3m wide 25mm deep 0.02m/s advance: 150); E31 (fix cutting power: specific energy 0.45 J/cm3 times 150 cm3/s: 67.5); E34 (tangential cutting force from 67.5W at 1 m/s tip speed: 67.5)

## Tracked vehicle ground pressure versus soft soil bearing
Rubber-track ground pressure is weight over contact area p = W/A. Wide tracks achieve single-digit kPa, for example 893 N over 0.32 m2 equals 2.79 kPa. This is an order of magnitude below generic soft-soil bearing values of 30-80 kPa, the regime where Bekker-type sinkage and slip-sinkage in dry loose sand become small.
Evidence: E18 (search: rubber track ground pressure soft soil bearing capacity kPa: wuxipolysource.com — Crawler-Mounted Drilling Rigs: Terrain Capability & Selection | PolySource — https://www.wuxipolysource.com/news_show/241.html); E19 (search: rubber track ground pressure soft soil bearing capacity kPa: docest.com — 2.1 Basic Definitions of Soft Soil — https://docest.com/doc/296838/2-1-basic-definitions-of-soft-soil); E20 (search: Bekker terramechanics sinkage track loose sand equation: c.coek.info — Effects of loading patterns on the pressure-sinkage relation of dry loose sand — https://c.coek.info/pdf-effects-of-loading-patterns-on-the-pressure-sinkage-relation-of-dry-loose-sand-.html); E21 (search: Bekker terramechanics sinkage track loose sand equation: spacecraft.ssl.umd.edu — 788XF22L23.tracks — https://spacecraft.ssl.umd.edu/academics/788XF22/788XF22L23.tracks.pdf); E24 (total loaded weight 60kg robot +31kg soil: 893 N); E25 (ground pressure loaded tracks: 2.79 kPa)

## 18650 Li-ion energy density for mobile robots
18650 Li-ion cells provide about 180-250 Wh per kg usable at small-pack level. A 43.3 Wh mission energy, for example 130 W for 20 min of drive plus digging plus lift plus compute, is carried by about 0.23-0.24 kg of cells before structure and electronics.

## Teleoperated robot link-loss stop distance
Wireless teleoperation practice and emergency-stop standards require automatic stop on link loss via deadman and watchdog. Stop distance equals creep distance during timeout plus braking, for example 0.3 m per s creep with 1 s watchdog stops in about 0.32 m.
Evidence: E37 (search: teleoperated mobile robot emergency stop link loss deadman standard: sourcebyspec.com — Wireless vs Hardwired E-Stop for Mobile Machines: Spec Rules and Trade-Offs — https://www.sourcebyspec.com/news/wireless-vs-hardwired-e-stop-for-mobile-machines-spec-rules-and-trade-offs.html); E38 (search: teleoperated mobile robot emergency stop link loss deadman standard: data.epo.org — EP 0960697 A1 19991201 - ROBOT EMERGENCY STOP DEADMAN SWITCH MECHANISM AND TEACHING CONTROL PANEL — https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP0960697A1-ROBOT-EMERGENCY-STOP-DEADMAN-SWITCH-MECHANISM-AND-TEACHING-CONTROL-PANEL.html); E42 (link-loss stop distance creep plus braking: 0.323 m)
