# Insights from Small wind turbine for a wind-tunnel competition
Source run: 20261103-105038_small-wind-turbine-for-a-wind-tunnel-com

## Sub-meter rotor power, speed and thrust regime
Sub-meter three-blade rotors in 5-11 m/s winds operate at low Reynolds numbers where SG6043-class sections are used. Representative regime: ~0.4 m diameter yields ~33 W shaft power at ~2626 rpm (~275 rad/s) at 11 m/s. Thrust follows dynamic pressure times disc area: running thrust coefficient ~0.9 gives ~33.5 N at 22 m/s and parked drag coefficient ~1.2 gives ~44.7 N, with q*A disc-load check ~37.3 N. Resulting support moments ~13.5 N-m produce tens of MPa stress in small 6061-T6 tube structures.
Evidence: E1 (search: small wind turbine tip speed ratio power coefficient 3 blade low Reynolds SG6043: atlantis-press.com — Experimental Study of Low-Speed Wind Turbine Performance with SG6043 Airfoil for Frontier Regions in Indonesia — https://atlantis-press.com/article/126024492.pdf); E9 (rotor power and gust thrust for 40cm rotor: V=11 om=275.0 rad/s rpm=2626; survival thrust CT0.9 = 33.5 N; parked drag Cd1.2 = 44.7 N); E15 (survival dynamic pressure times disc area: 37.3); E25 (shaft power from torque and speed: 33)

## Outrunner Kv sets wild voltage range
Brushless outrunner machines operated as direct-drive generators produce open-circuit voltage proportional to shaft speed divided by Kv rating. For ~220 Kv class, high-speed output ~11.9 V and low-wind output ~5.43 V, roughly a 2:1 wild AC voltage spread requiring buck-boost regulation to a stable output. Power transfer across yaw by slip-ring and cable is current limited at the ~4 A class.
Evidence: E17 (search: brushless outrunner motor Kv rating generator small wind turbine: hobbyking.com — Scorpion S-5030-220kv (F3A Special) Brushless Outrunner Motor — https://hobbyking.com/en_us/scorpion-s-5030-220kv-f3a-special-brushless-outrunner-motor.html); E23 (open-circuit volts at top speed for 220 Kv generator: 11.9); E24 (open-circuit volts at 5 m/s low speed: 5.43)

## Rectifier loss and electrical budget limits
Three-phase Schottky bridges conduct through two diodes at any instant. At ~3 A this is ~3.3 W conduction loss, reducing ~33 W shaft power to ~21.4 W net electrical before DC-DC conversion. Dump-resistor braking and rectifier losses appear as short high-power pulses, e.g. ~47.2 W at 3 ohm versus ~70.8 W at 2 ohm for ~11.9 V bus, requiring heatsinking and enclosure venting.
Evidence: E19 (search: three phase Schottky rectifier module datasheet voltage drop: mouser.com — SCHOTTKY RECTIFIER 3 .0 Amp — http://www.mouser.com/catalog/specsheets/94313mbr.pdf); E44 (diode conduction loss two diodes at 3A: 3.3 W); E47 (net electrical before DC-DC stage: 21.4); E48 (dump power 3 ohm at 11.9V: 47.2)

## Small-turbine safety standard and fail-safe stop physics
IEC 61400-2 governs small wind turbines for survival wind loading, electrical safety including open-circuit voltage limits, and safety shutdown with braking to stop and hold at zero speed. Brake physics for light rotors: stored spin energy is tens of joules (~75.6 J); required average torque to reach 10% speed in 10 s is ~0.0495 N-m for inertia ~0.002 kg-m2, while spring-applied friction can hold ~2.24 N-m for ~45x margin. Short-circuit plus dump-resistor braking with ~3 ohm limits current to ~3.97 A. Tail-vane yaw stability requires restoring moment to exceed aerodynamic disturbance, e.g. 0.69-0.834 N-m versus ~0.67 N-m for margin ~1.0-1.24, set by fin area and boom length.
