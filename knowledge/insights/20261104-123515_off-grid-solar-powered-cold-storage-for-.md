## 100 mm PIR/PUR sandwich envelope U-value at hot-region delta-T
For insulated cold rooms, 100 mm PIR with lambda approx 0.022 W/m-K gives U approx 0.22 to 0.25 W/m2K. Transmission load = U * area * delta-T; example at delta-T 39 K (43 C ambient to 4 C interior) on ~25.6 m2 skin is ~220-250 W, ~5.27 kWh/day thermal, ~2.1 kWh/day electric at COP 2.5. Panels are typically specified to EN 14509 for self-supporting double-skin metal-faced construction, B-s1,d0 fire class, with taped cam-lock joints and continuous warm-side vapour barrier to maintain U and prevent interstitial condensation.

## Produce pull-down versus steady holding energy scales
Pull-down dominates holding in small stores. For fresh produce cp approx 3.9 kJ/kg-K, cooling 1 t through 31 K requires about 33.6 kWh thermal, about 13.4 kWh electric at COP 2.5, equivalent to ~6 days of wall transmission alone. Steady holding for a ~8 m3 gross room at 43 C ambient is on order 3.5-3.6 kWh/day electric, comprising transmission plus produce respiration (~30 W average per tonne, ~0.72 kWh/day thermal, ~0.29 kWh/day electric at COP 2.5) plus fan and <2 ACH infiltration. FAO horticulture stores typically operate 0-10 C, so similar delta-T regime applies.

## Small DC hermetic vapour-compression class for off-grid cold rooms
Small hermetic DC compressors (R134a/R600a BD-class, 12/24/48 V variable-speed monoblock) operated at about 4 C evaporating / 43 C condensing deliver ~0.6 kW cooling at COP 2.2-2.5, with steady holding draw 150-270 W and peak ~600 W. Eliminating DC-AC inversion avoids ~8% additional loss and high surge current. This class defines the electric load that PV/battery must meet.

## Floor load vs. XPS insulation compressive strength
Total ground pressure for a 1-tonne produce cold store (produce plus enclosure, racks, plant, skid) is typically 3-4.3 kPa on 4.0-5.8 m2 footprint. Industrial XPS floor insulation for cold stores is commonly rated ~300 kPa compressive strength at 10% deformation with limits on long-term creep. Even with plywood load spreading on a steel skid, the applied pressure is ~1% of short-term rating, leaving margin but requiring assessment against long-term creep under sustained load.

## PV/battery and infiltration rule of thumb in high-PSH off-grid service
In sub-Saharan Africa peak sun hours are ~5 PSH with usable PV yield ~kWp * PSH * 0.70 system efficiency (MPPT plus wiring). Minimum kWp = daily electric load / (PSH * 0.70); holding of ~3.6 kWh/day needs ~1.03 kWp before pull-down and autonomy margins. LiFePO4 banks at 48 V 100 Ah provide 4.8 kWh nominal, 3.84 kWh usable at 80% DOD, so a 14 h night at 150 W (2.1 kWh) is ~55% of usable capacity. Uncontrolled infiltration dominates if doors are poorly sealed; a 0.9x1.8 m gasket door with PVC strip, self-closer and threshold can hold <2 ACH and 0.5-1.0 kWh/day infiltration, versus large penalty for open-room operation.
