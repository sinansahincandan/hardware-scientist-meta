Source run: 20261104-121011_tactile-fingertip-sensor-for-robot-hands

## Millinewton Hertz contact on soft silicone
Silicone skin with modulus ~0.435 MPa (Shore A ~10 class) under 1 mN normal load forms a Hertz contact of ~0.235 mm radius (~0.47 mm diameter spot) at ~5.78 kPa mean pressure. Spot diameter scales as (load/modulus)^{1/3}; millinewton thresholds therefore present sub-millimeter pressure spots and single-digit kPa signals to any underlying transducer.

## Capacitive elastomer taxel sensitivity regime
A 1.5 mm parallel-plate elastomer taxel with ~200 um gap has ~0.269 pF base capacitance; ~5.8 kPa compression gives ~0.013 strain and only ~3.57 fF change. Detecting this needs capacitance-to-digital conversion with sub-femtofarad resolution (~244 aF/LSB class for ~14 counts margin). The same elastomer dielectric stays below ~0.49 strain to ~213 kPa at 50 N, defining a usable dual-range regime of ~5 kPa to >200 kPa without saturation.

## Incipient-slip vibration band and latency budget
Tangential stick-to-slip produces a micro-vibration burst around 200 Hz in the 100-500 Hz Pacinian band at ~1 um amplitude, with micro-slip onset to gross slip spanning ~53.6 to 96.8 ms (~43 ms gap). Closing a warning inside that gap requires ~30 ms observation window sampled at ~2.2 kHz; a 44-tap band filter at that rate costs only ~96.8 kMAC/s. Frame updates near 9-10 ms cannot carry the 100-500 Hz band.
Evidence: E38 (stick-to-slip transition vs time: shear ramp to Coulomb limit plus 200 Hz micro-vibration burst and 30 ms detector window: t_microslip_ms=53.6 t_grossslip_ms=96.8; vib_um=1.0 f_Hz=200 detector_window_ms=30 pwr_end=2.54e-13; stick_ratio at 0,50,100ms: ['0.95', '0.72', '0.48']); E84 (slip warning gap vs detector window: 43.2); E78 (FIR multiply-accumulate rate for 44-tap slip filter at 2200 Hz: 96800); E34 (search: PVDF piezoelectric slip sensor robot fingertip high frequency: overfitted.cloud — Contact Status Recognition and Slip Detection with a Bio-inspired Tactile Hand — https://overfitted.cloud/pdf/2603.18370v1)

## Finger grip force from friction and contact pressure
With Coulomb friction mu 0.5-1.0 for silicone/skin on dry objects, holding 1 kg with two fingers needs ~9.81 N normal per finger at mu 0.5, or ~125 kPa over ~5 mm radius contact, scaling as weight/(fingers*mu). Power grips to 50 N reach ~213 kPa. Reference envelope for this force regime is a human-scale fingertip ~20 mm diameter x 22 mm long within 18x28 mm finger bounds.
