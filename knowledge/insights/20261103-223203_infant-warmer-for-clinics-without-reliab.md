## IEC 60601-1 skin-contact thermal limit for prolonged contact
IEC 60601-1 Clause 11 sets maximum skin-contact surface temperature of 43 degC for contact >10 min; temperatures >41 degC require justification. Design practice applies lower internal limits (e.g., 40 degC) to maintain margin under worst-case charge conditions.

## Paraffin PCM at 37 degC latent heat and energy density
Paraffin phase-change materials tuned to ~37 degC provide latent heat ~180-210 J/g. At 200 J/g this equals ~55.6 Wh per kg of PCM. Total stored energy is mass times latent heat (e.g., 1.2 kg x 210 J/g = 252 kJ = 70 Wh), with usable fraction to target typically ~50% after ambient losses.

## Neonatal normothermia band and thermal power budget
WHO defines newborn normothermia as 36.5 to 37.5 degC, a 1 K control band. Maintenance in 20-25 degC ambient requires on order 5 W continuous to the infant, or ~20 Wh for 4 h. Lumped models must account for parallel loss to ambient (~4 W at 22 degC in enclosed configurations) when sizing storage.

## Infant bassinet dimensional and handling constraints
16 CFR Part 1218 defines safety requirements for bassinets and cradles, with common internal surfaces around 60x35 cm and overall footprint ~65x40 cm to fit cots/tables. For portable medical devices, one-person and one-hand carry guidance (e.g., Ohio BWC, ISO 11228-1) limits device mass to approximately <5-6 kg and constrains wall height and stability against tip/roll.

## WHO infection-control material requirement for low-resource devices
Reusable infant-contact surfaces must tolerate cleaning with soap/water and disinfection with 0.5% chlorine solution between patients, typically validated for >50 wipe cycles. Requires seamless or coated polymers (e.g., ABS/PE, PUL-coated textiles) and removable liners to avoid fluid traps.
