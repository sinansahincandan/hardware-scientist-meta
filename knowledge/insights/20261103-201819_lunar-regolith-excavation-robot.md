# Insights from Lunar regolith excavation robot
Source run: 20261103-201819_lunar-regolith-excavation-robot

## BP-1 Lunar Simulant Shear Strength Regime
Black Point Basalt (BP-1) loose lunar simulant exhibits peak internal friction angles of 39 to 51 degrees and cohesion of 0.2 to 0.5 kPa at low bulk density. This high-friction, low-cohesion regime governs both tool draft and bulk flow/gravity discharge behavior.

## Bekker Pressure-Sinkage Controls For Small Rigid Wheels On Lunar Simulant
Bearing capacity and sinkage of small rigid wheels on lunar simulant is described by Bekker pressure-sinkage model p = (k_c/b + k_phi)*z^n with simulant-specific k_c, k_phi, n from bevameter tests. At ~11.5 kPa bearing in loose BP-1, 120-250 mm diameter grouser wheels develop sinkage and rolling resistance that limits usable drawbar to mu ~0.30 * normal weight, requiring traction draft reserve >1.5 for reliable traverse.

## McKyes Narrow-Blade Cutting Draft In Cohesive-Frictional Soil
Draft force of narrow scraper/blade tools in lunar simulant follows McKyes cutting model based on cohesion, friction angle (39-51 deg), surcharge, rake angle and cut depth. Draft rises approximately linearly with depth over 10 to 25 mm (order 40 N at 10 mm to >100 N at 25 mm for ~300 mm wide blade) and can be reduced by low-amplitude vibration (~25 Hz, few mm).

## Gravity Discharge Requires Wall Angle > Peak Friction Angle
For reliable gravity flow without bridging rat-holing, sealed hopper/bin walls must be steeper than the simulant peak friction angle. For BP-1 with 39-51 deg friction, walls >=50 deg from horizontal are required for gravity discharge, reducing effective volumetric utilisation to ~30-35% of bounding box.

## Teleoperated Small Robot Power And Link Architecture
Small field robots use 6S Li-ion ~22.2 V battery with Battery Management System and fused distribution bus for combined drive, actuation and sensing loads (~200 Wh class for <60 kg platforms). Remote operation uses 5 GHz WiFi with dual antennas and <0.5 s link-loss watchdog; mast-mounted LiDAR/camera (deployed ~800 mm) provides line-of-sight for fill-level and obstacle detection.
