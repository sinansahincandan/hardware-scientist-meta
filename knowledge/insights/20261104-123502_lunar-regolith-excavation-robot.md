# Insights from Lunar regolith excavation robot
Source run: 20261104-123502_lunar-regolith-excavation-robot

## BP-1 simulant bearing regime
BP-1 basaltic lunar simulant is a dense, high-friction granular material with friction angle ~39-51 deg and low cohesion typically 0.5-1 kPa, rising to ~5 kPa when compacted or silt-paste. Bearing behaviour is pressure-sinkage controlled, not hard-rock cutting.

## Bekker pressure-sinkage scaling for wheels on simulant
Wheel sinkage on BP-1 follows Bekker p = (kc/b + kphi) * z^n and contact length l = 2*sqrt(D*z - z^2). At Earth weight ~589 N, ground pressure ~7-11 kPa on 65-100 mm contact gives 4-6 mm sinkage for 300 mm dia wheels; traction capacity then is T = c*A + W*tan(phi) ~500-570 N or simplified mu*W ~235 N.

## McKyes cutting draft dominated by cohesion
Excavation draft in regolith follows McKyes F = w*[gamma*d^2*N_gamma + c*d*N_c]. For a 0.4 m wide blade at 15-30 mm depth, the weight term is ~16 N per blade while cohesion at 1 kPa adds ~24 N per blade giving ~80 N twin-drum total; at 5 kPa cohesion the cohesion term ~120 N per blade drives twin total to ~273 N, exceeding typical wheeled traction and defining a no-go paste limit.

## Sealed bay thermal and IP6X dust regime
IEC 60529 IP6X dust-tight for sub-10 µm BP-1 fines is verified by talc chamber test and in practice requires labyrinth 0.6±0.2 mm over ~12 mm plus static O-ring squeeze 15-25% (nominal 21.8%) and 0.2 um ePTFE vent. In a sealed Li-ion/LiFePO4 bay, usable energy is ~60% of nameplate (e.g. 240 Wh to 144 Wh at 20-80% SoC), and thermal rise is dominated by gap filler and chassis interface (~0.85 K/W total) not the aluminium spreader plate (~0.00028 K/W for 3 mm Al), giving ~21 K rise at ~25 W hold.
